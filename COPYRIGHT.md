# Copyright, fair dealing, and what is actually claimed here

## The position, in one table

| Layer | Whose | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| The broadcasts | Mriya Report and the speakers | **No claim made.** Reproduced under fair dealing |
| The transcript text — what was said | Mriya Report and the speakers | **No claim made.** Reproduced under fair dealing |
| Analysis, commentary, annotation, findings | Prime Rogue Inc. | CC BY-NC 4.0 |
| Index, structure, metadata, checksums, correction log | Prime Rogue Inc. | CC BY-NC 4.0 |

**Prime Rogue Inc. asserts no ownership of the transcript text.** A transcript of someone
else's speech is their speech. The transcription labour is real, and it is not being
converted into a property claim over what other people said.

That is a narrower claim than this kind of repository usually makes, and it is deliberate.
It is also the easier position to defend: there is no argument to be had about whether a
transcript creates a new copyright, because no such right is being asserted.

## The fair dealing basis

Copyright Act ss. 29–29.2 — research, private study, criticism, review and **news
reporting**. This material supports published journalism about the Mriya Aid and Mriya
Report organisations and is relied on for that purpose.

Fair dealing for criticism, review and news reporting is **conditional on attribution** of
the source and, where the source gives it, the author, performer, maker or broadcaster.
**A transcript stripped of its attribution loses the basis for the dealing.** Attribution
is therefore structural here, not a courtesy.

## Source and preservation purpose

Recordings are obtained from the **Mriya Report's own public YouTube channel.**

This repository exists in part because **material by the same publisher has been removed
from other platforms.** Preserving a public broadcast that is being withdrawn from public
view, for the purpose of reporting on the organisation that made it, is squarely within the
public-interest purpose fair dealing serves.

**Where removal is asserted, it is documented**: the prior location, the date last seen,
and the archival record establishing that it existed. "They deleted it" is a claim and
carries a basis like any other.

## Minimisation — who appears and who does not

Live broadcasts carry people who are not the subject of anything.

- **Principals and public figures, speaking publicly in a public capacity:** named.
- **Incidental participants, callers and members of the public:** **not named.** Rendered
  `[participant]` where needed for sense, omitted where not.
- **Personal information in passing** — addresses, contact details, family, health, private
  life — **not transcribed**, and the omission marked.

A public transcript repository is the most exposed artifact in any investigation. These
rules are what make it defensible.

## Takedown and correction

Rights holders, and anyone named, may write to **kevin@primerogueinc.com**.

Accuracy corrections are made against the recording and entered in the correction log, never
silently edited. A rights-holder complaint is answered on the merits.

**A takedown request from a subject of reporting is not automatically honoured**, and the
reason is in the previous paragraph of this document: material withdrawn from public view by
the people being reported on is the specific thing this repository exists to preserve. Each
request is considered individually.
